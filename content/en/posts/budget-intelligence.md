Following up on [The Cost of Intelligence]({{< relref "/posts/cost-of-intelligence" >}}), this post zooms into the sub-$2 range — the price bracket that matters most for hobbyists, indie developers, and small teams.

The previous post showed that the trend of cheap models getting smarter is real. But the slider went up to $20. Here I want to focus on the range where most of us actually operate: under two dollars per million tokens.

At this price point you're looking at about $2-40 per month for typical coding usage (1M tokens/day, 20 working days). That's the difference between "let me try this AI" and "this is a regular part of my workflow."

{{< llm-cheap >}}

At $1/M tokens we already have models with an intelligence index above 50 — that's genuinely useful for coding assistance, refactoring, and technical Q&A. As the exponential trend plays out, we'll likely see frontier-level intelligence trickle down into this bracket within months.

What I find interesting is the open-weight competition in this range. DeepSeek, Qwen, and others are making capable models available at commodity prices. This is the range where running your own inference might actually make sense financially.

The sub-$1 range is thinner on data, but the trend line suggests we'll see capable coding models at $0.5/M tokens by late 2026. At that price, AI assistance becomes essentially frictionless — you stop thinking about token costs entirely.
